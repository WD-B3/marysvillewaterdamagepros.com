Water damage restoration is one job on the surface and many jobs underneath. The cause of the water, the room it reached and the material it soaked all change what happens next, so this site gives each situation its own page. The main water damage restoration page explains the whole process, and the pages below go deeper on each cause.

Pipes and plumbing

Pressurized lines fail differently from drains, so the cleanup starts at the source. A burst pipe floods fast, a frozen pipe often floods when it thaws, a water heater leak usually pools in a utility space, and a slab leak hides under the floor until the signs show up above it.

Appliances and HVAC

Most appliance floods come from a hose, a valve or a drain line, not from the appliance itself. Read about an appliance leak, an AC leak from a blocked condensate line, or kitchen water damage in cabinets and under sinks.

Weather and the building envelope

Water that enters from outside needs a different assessment from water that starts inside. A ceiling leak may begin on the roof, storm water damage covers wind and rain getting in, flood damage covers rising water, and the lower levels have their own pages for basements and crawl spaces.

Water removal and drying

Standing water comes out first with water extraction. Then structural drying brings walls and floors back to a dry standard. When the water came from a drain or toilet, sewage backup cleanup adds contamination control and sanitizing.

Floors and walls

Materials dry at different speeds. See what happens to hardwood floors, how carpet and pad are judged, and when wall and drywall repair is needed after the drying is done.

Commercial properties

Businesses lose money while they are closed, so commercial water damage restoration puts the schedule, the tenants and the insurance paperwork alongside the drying plan.

Not sure which page fits?

Many losses involve two or three of these at once, such as a burst pipe that soaks a ceiling and a carpet. That is normal.
