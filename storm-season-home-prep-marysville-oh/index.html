How to Prepare Your Marysville, OH Home for Storm Season

Most storm water losses start with something small that was not checked. A short list handles most of it.

(740) 231-9132See all services

By the Marysville Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

Storm season is easier on a home that has been walked through in advance. The checks below take an afternoon and focus on where water enters or backs up: the roof, the gutters, the foundation, the basement and the plumbing. They are general steps for any Ohio house, and they are written for a homeowner, not a contractor.

Start at the roof and gutters

From the ground, look for missing or lifted shingles, loose flashing around vents and chimneys, and sagging gutters. Clean gutters and downspouts so water does not spill down the walls. Make sure downspouts discharge several feet from the foundation, because water that pools beside a basement wall tends to find its way in.

Check the grade and the foundation

The soil beside the house should slope away from it. Fill low spots, keep mulch from covering weep holes and look at the foundation for cracks that leak after rain. Window wells need clean gravel or drains, and covers help keep out leaves and water. Small fixes here cost far less than a wet finished basement.

Test the sump pump and plan for outages

Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and watch the pump start and shut off. Check the discharge line for blockages and make sure it ends outside, away from the house. Storms often cause power outages, which is when a pump is needed most. A battery backup is worth discussing with a plumber.

Know your shutoffs and your documents

Find the main water valve and make sure it turns. Know where the electrical panel is. Photograph rooms and valuables for an inventory, and keep your insurance policy where you can reach it. Ask your agent whether your policy covers water backup and what flood coverage would involve.

If water gets in despite your efforts, call early. See the pages on basement water damage and flood damage cleanup for what the response looks like.

Inside the house before the storm

Move valuables, boxes and electronics off the basement floor and onto shelves or plastic bins. Unplug small electronics in lower levels if a storm is forecast. Check that the floor drain is clear and that the sump pit is clear of debris. If the water heater, furnace or electrical panel sits in a low area, raising stored items around them makes cleanup easier if water does enter.

After the storm passes

Walk the exterior in daylight and look for downed branches on the roof, loose shingles, clogged downspouts and new puddles near the foundation. Inside, check ceilings and the basement for new stains or damp spots. A stain that appears a day or two after a storm often means water found a path through the roof or wall, and it is much easier to deal with when it is small.

If you find standing water, stay away from outlets, appliances and the electrical panel while water is present. Photograph the damage before moving anything, shut off the water and electricity to the affected area if it is safe to do so and call for help. The sooner water comes out and drying starts, the more of the structure and contents can be saved.

Keep a storm kit in one place

  • Flashlight and spare batteries.
  • Phone charger or battery pack.
  • A copy of your insurance policy and agent phone number.
  • Plastic sheeting and tape for temporary roof or window cover.
  • Photos or a video inventory of your belongings.

Neighbors are a resource. Ask the people on your street who have lived there a long time where water tends to pool and which storms caused trouble. Their experience is often more specific than any map, and it tells you where to look first. Share your own notes in return, especially if you find a fix that works.

Key takeaways

  • Gutters, grade and window wells decide where rain goes.
  • Test the sump pump and think about backup power.
  • Raise valuables off basement floors before heavy rain.
  • Walk the exterior after every big storm.

Safety steps before you touch anything

  • Never touch a wet outlet, panel or appliance, and turn off power at the panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground.
  • Keep a generator outdoors and away from windows.
  • Stay out of basements with standing water until the electricity is off or confirmed safe.

Professional water damage work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets out the water categories and the drying goals crews work toward. If anyone is at risk from electricity, gas or contaminated water, deal with that first and call dispatch afterward.

Local note for Marysville, OH homeowners

Mill Creek runs through Marysville, and Union County has documented logjams in the creek. In 2017 the county found a logjam about 1,600 feet long, and its project page says logjams act like dams that can back up water and slow stormwater drainage. The county identified 759 logjams or debris piles, and the city has 299 parcels along the creek.

The city lists stormwater under its Engineering Department. If you have a drainage concern near your property, ask the city. In Plain City, the village owns its own storm sewers and outfalls to Darby Creek, and in Raymond you would contact the county.
