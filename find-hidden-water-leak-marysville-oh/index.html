How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your Marysville, OH House

A hidden leak wastes water and damages materials quietly. A meter check and a walk-through find most of them.

(740) 231-9132See all services

By the Marysville Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

Not every leak announces itself with a puddle. Some run inside walls, under floors or in a slab for weeks. The signs are subtle: a rising water bill, a musty smell, a warm spot on the floor or a faint sound of running water when nothing is on.

This guide gives a simple order of checks you can do yourself, and explains when to bring in a plumber or a restoration crew with detection tools.

Step 1: Do a meter test

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and the ice maker so no water is in use.
  2. Find your water meter and look at the leak indicator, a small dial or triangle, or note the read.
  3. Wait 30 minutes without using water, then look again.
  4. If the dial moved or the number changed, water is flowing somewhere.
  5. Close the main valve and check again. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house.

Step 2: Check the usual suspects

Toilets cause many hidden leaks. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait 15 minutes. If color shows in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking. Check under sinks, behind the refrigerator ice line, around the dishwasher and washing machine hoses, and around the water heater. Look at hose bibs and irrigation lines outside.

Step 3: Look and listen for signs

  • Stains, bubbling paint or warped baseboards.
  • Soft, spongy or buckled flooring.
  • Musty odor with no visible source.
  • Hot or damp spots on a slab floor, which can suggest a slab leak.
  • The sound of running water in a quiet house.
  • Unexplained green patches or wet ground in the yard along the service line.

When to call for help

If the meter moves with all fixtures off and you cannot find the cause, call a plumber for leak detection. If you find damage, call a restoration crew to measure moisture and dry the area. A leak that is fixed but leaves wet materials behind will continue to cause damage. The slab leak page and the crawl space page describe two places leaks often go unnoticed.

Photograph anything you find and keep a record of readings. If the damage may be a covered loss, those notes help later. Repair the source before drying, since drying a wet area that is still being fed is wasted effort.

Using the bill as a clue

Compare your last few water bills. A steady increase with no change in habits points to a slow leak, and a sudden jump points to a larger failure. Compare the same months in different years to account for seasonal use. Your utility can often provide usage history, which shows the pattern more clearly than a single bill.

What not to do

  • Do not cut into walls at random, since the wet spot is often far from the leak.
  • Do not paint over a stain before the source is found.
  • Do not run drying equipment on a wet area that is still being fed by a leak.
  • Do not ignore a warm spot on a slab floor.

Detection tools help avoid guesswork. Plumbers use acoustic listening devices and pressure tests to locate leaks in pipes. Restoration crews use moisture meters and thermal cameras to map wet materials. Using both gives a picture of where water is coming from and where it has gone.

After the leak is found

Once the plumber repairs the source, the wet materials still need to be dried. A repaired pipe in a wet wall is not the end of the job. Ask for readings that show the wall is dry before it is closed and painted. Keep the plumber's invoice and the readings together, since they document both the cause and the cure.

Keep a short log while you search. Write down the meter reading, the time and what was running. A second reading a few hours later, and another overnight, shows whether the flow is constant or only happens when something is used. A constant flow suggests a line under pressure, while an on and off pattern points to a fixture such as a toilet or a softener.

Key takeaways

  • Run a meter test with all water off for 30 minutes.
  • Check toilets, hoses and appliances first.
  • Warm slab spots and constant meter movement need professional detection.
  • Dry the area after the source is repaired.

Local note for Marysville, OH homeowners

If the meter test points to a leak but the house is dry inside, the problem may be on the service line. The City of Marysville Public Service Department oversees the Water and Wastewater divisions, and the Utility Billing office can help you read your usage history. Ask who is responsible for each part of the line before you dig.
