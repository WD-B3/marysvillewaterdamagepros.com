How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Marysville, OH

Knowing where the valve is and that it turns can turn a flood into a small cleanup. Check it this week.

(740) 231-9132See all services

By the Marysville Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

When a pipe lets go, the most valuable minute is the first one. A homeowner who can find and turn the main shutoff valve stops new water from entering the house, which limits how much extraction and drying is needed afterward. Most people never look for the valve until the emergency, and that is when they discover it is blocked, stuck or unmarked.

This guide walks through where to look, how to turn it off and what to check before you need it.

Where the main valve usually is

The main shutoff is where the water line enters the house. In a home with a basement or crawl space, it is often on the wall facing the street, near the water meter, close to where the pipe comes through the foundation. In a slab home, it may be in a utility room, a garage, a closet near the water heater or in a box outside.

Some homes also have a shutoff at the meter that belongs to the water utility, and you should not operate that one unless the utility tells you to.

Look for a round wheel handle (a gate valve) or a lever handle (a ball valve). A lever parallel to the pipe means open, and a lever across the pipe means closed. A wheel turns clockwise to close. If you cannot find the valve, trace the line from the water heater back toward the wall, or follow the meter.

How to shut it off

  1. Turn the handle slowly. A wheel valve closes clockwise, and a lever turns a quarter turn.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain what remains in the pipes.
  3. Open a faucet at the highest point to let air in so the lines drain.
  4. Check that the water meter dial or low-flow indicator has stopped moving.
  5. Leave the valve closed and call a plumber for the repair.

Turn the valve gently. Old gate valves can corrode and break, and forcing one can make the leak worse. If it is stiff, a little steady pressure is fine. If it will not move, do not use a wrench with a long handle, because a broken stem can leave you with no way to stop the water at all.

Test your valve once a year

Valves that are never used tend to seize. Once a year, close the main, check that water stops at a faucet and open it again. If it leaks around the stem, drips or will not close fully, a plumber can replace it before you need it.

Also locate the shutoffs under sinks and behind toilets, because closing a single fixture is faster than shutting off the whole house for a small failure.

Label the valve with a tag and make sure everyone in the household knows where it is. Keep the area clear so you can reach it in the dark. Write down where the electrical panel is as well, because water and power need to be dealt with together.

After the water is off

Shutting off the main stops new water, but the water already on the floor still needs to come out. If it is deep, stay away from outlets and appliances, and call dispatch. The water extraction page explains what the first visit looks like, and the burst pipe page covers what happens after a line fails. Take photos before cleanup begins, because you may want them later for an insurance claim.

If the valve is old or hard to reach

Many older homes have a gate valve that has not moved in years. If yours is stiff, leaks at the stem or has a handle that spins loosely, ask a plumber about replacing it with a quarter turn ball valve.

The job is usually short, and a modern valve is much more likely to work on the day you need it. If the valve is behind storage or in a cramped corner, clear a path now.

Teach everyone in the household

The best valve is useless if only one person knows where it is. Walk everyone old enough through the steps, show them the tag on the valve and write the location on a card near the fridge or in the contact list on your phone.

Show the babysitter, the house sitter and the neighbor who checks in when you travel. A two minute walk-through is worth more than any amount of reading.

Also learn which fixtures have their own shutoffs. Toilets, sinks, the dishwasher and the washing machine usually have small valves nearby. Closing one of those stops a local failure without cutting water to the entire house, and it leaves the rest of the home usable while you wait for a plumber.

Make a short emergency card

  • Location of the main water valve.
  • Location of the electrical panel.
  • Your insurance agent and policy number.
  • The plumber you trust.
  • The 24/7 dispatch number for water removal.

If water is already in the house, do not wait on the paperwork. Call (740) 231-9132.

Key takeaways

  • Find the main valve before you need it.
  • Wheel valves close clockwise and lever valves close a quarter turn.
  • Test the valve once a year and replace one that sticks.
  • Make sure everyone in the home knows where it is.

Local note for Marysville, OH homeowners

The City of Marysville Public Service Department oversees the Water and Wastewater divisions, and the city has a Utility Billing office for account questions. If the leak is between the meter and the street, that part of the line is a question for the city, not for a plumber, so call Public Service first. For anything inside the house, the shutoff is yours to operate.

Homes just outside the city, including those in Raymond and Plain City, may be served by different providers or private wells, so ask your supplier where the shutoff and meter are before you need them.
