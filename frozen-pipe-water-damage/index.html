Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Marysville, OH

Ice splits a pipe, but the water often arrives when it thaws. Dispatch is open 24/7 for the cleanup.

A frozen pipe becomes an emergency the moment it thaws and the split lets go. If you suspect a pipe is frozen or has already cracked, shut off the main water valve, open the nearest faucet, and call the 24/7 dispatch line. Do not use an open flame to thaw a pipe. If water is already on the floor, keep people out of it and stay away from outlets until the area is safe.

This page covers damage caused by water that froze inside a line and split it. If a pipe simply let go under pressure with no freezing, see burst pipe water damage, and for the water itself, water extraction explains what happens next.

Frozen pipe emergency: the thaw is when flooding starts

Ice in a pipe acts like a plug. When it freezes, it expands and can split the pipe along its length, but water may not escape until the ice melts.

Many frozen pipe calls come on the first warm day after a cold stretch, when a pipe that looked fine starts spraying inside a wall or ceiling. That is why damp spots, hissing sounds or a sudden drop in water pressure after a freeze deserve a call even if nothing is dripping.

  • Shut off the main water valve and open a faucet to relieve pressure.
  • If you can see the frozen section and it is not cracked, warm the room with a space heater placed safely away from anything wet.
  • Never use a torch, a propane heater or an open flame on a pipe.
  • Look for wet ceilings, bulging drywall and dripping from light fixtures, and keep away from any wet fixture.
  • Call dispatch if you see water, hear running water you cannot explain or notice a sagging ceiling.

Where pipes freeze in Ohio homes

Pipes freeze where heat does not reach: exterior walls, unheated crawl spaces and garages, attics, and runs along uninsulated rim joists. Winters here regularly drop below freezing overnight, and a pipe that survives one cold night can fail on the next if wind and temperature fall together. Outdoor spigots and the lines feeding them are a frequent weak point. Homes that were vacant over a cold stretch can lose a pipe unnoticed for days.

What the cleanup involves

The crew finds the split, confirms the water is off and maps moisture with a thermal camera and meters. Frozen pipe losses often wet more of the structure than people expect, because water travels inside walls and across ceilings before anyone notices. Standing water is extracted, wet insulation and drywall that cannot dry in place are removed, and air movers and dehumidifiers go in.

A plumber replaces the damaged line and checks nearby runs that may have been stressed. Drywall, trim and flooring are repaired once readings show dry. Ceiling damage from a frozen line is covered in more detail on the ceiling water damage page.

Preventing a repeat

Insulating exposed lines, sealing drafts near pipes, letting a faucet drip on the coldest nights and draining outdoor spigots before winter all help. If you travel, keep the heat on and ask someone to check the house. These are general steps, and a plumber can tell you which runs in your home are most exposed.

What affects the price after a freeze

These are the things that move the number once the crew can see the damage.

  • Where the split is. A split in an exposed basement run is simpler than one inside a finished wall.
  • Time between thaw and discovery. Water that ran for hours reaches more rooms.
  • Ceilings and insulation. Wet ceilings and attic insulation add removal and disposal.
  • Drying duration. Cold, damp structures can take longer to bring to a dry standard.
  • Rebuild scope. Repairs follow drying and are priced separately.
