Where Adena Pointe is

Adena Pointe is a residential development in the City of Marysville, reached from Weaver Road. Fischer Homes builds there and lists it as a current community, and the Union County Airport Authority and the City of Marysville have both been named in local reporting about drainage at the site. If your address is on one of the newer streets, say so when you call.

What local reporting says about stormwater here

In March 2025 the Journal-Tribune reported that stormwater was pooling in water basins at Adena Pointe, and that the city and the airport authority were discussing improvements. Drawings in that report showed the Adena Pointe basin linked by a swale to a larger basin so water would move to the larger one and avoid flooding into the surrounding field.

That is a drainage fix in a development, not a report that houses flooded. We do not describe Adena Pointe as flood-prone and we found no record of home flooding. What the reporting does show is that stormwater handling here has been an active question, which is a good reason to watch your yard and foundation after heavy rain.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified for this page. Look up your own address on the FEMA flood map and ask the Union County Engineer, the county floodplain administrator, what applies.

New construction and water

New homes carry new warranties, and that matters. If water damage may relate to construction, such as a window, a roof flashing or a foundation issue, your builder or warranty provider may need to be told early. We can dry the structure and document what we find, and we do not decide who is responsible.

Window and roof leaks

Rain driven against a wall can find a flaw in flashing or caulk. See storm water damage cleanup.

Basement seepage

Water along a wall or floor joint after heavy rain needs source and drying attention. See basement cleanup.

Plumbing failures

Supply lines, tanks and hoses fail in new houses too. See burst pipe cleanup.

Wet drywall and trim

Finished walls need readings before they are closed up. See wall and drywall repair.

Safety before cleanup

Outside water that has been in a yard, a basin or a drain can carry contamination. Keep children and pets out of it, do not wade through it to reach a panel and do not use household fans or a vacuum on it. If power is off and the floor is wet, wait for professional help to assess the electrical risk.

Drying a newer house

Newer homes are built tighter, so moisture that stays inside takes longer to leave. Dehumidifiers and air movers do most of the work, and a crew should log readings on each visit until they match dry reference points. See structural drying for what that includes.

Documenting for insurance and warranty

Photograph the damage, the source and the date. Keep a log of rain and when you noticed the water. Share the photos with your insurer and, if construction may be involved, with your builder. We cannot promise how either decides, and what each covers depends on the policy and the warranty.
