Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained in Marysville

The category tells a crew how risky the water is and what can be saved. It can also change as time passes.

(740) 231-9132See all services

By the Marysville Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

Restoration crews describe water by category, which is a way of rating how contaminated it is. The categories are used across the industry, and they decide how much can be dried and how much has to be removed.

Knowing the basics helps you understand an estimate and ask better questions. The classification is made on site by the crew, so use this guide to understand the idea and not to diagnose your own situation.

Category 1: clean water

Category 1 comes from a sanitary source, such as a broken supply line, a faucet that was left on or a failed water heater tank. It is not harmful at first. But it does not stay that way. As it sits, absorbs soil from materials and warms, it can degrade into a higher category. Fast action is the reason clean-water losses are the best case.

Category 2: gray water

Category 2 contains contamination that could cause illness if swallowed or exposed to the skin. Typical sources are washing machine and dishwasher discharge, sump pump overflow and toilet overflow with urine but no feces. Porous materials such as carpet padding are usually removed, and other materials are cleaned and treated before drying.

Category 3: black water

Category 3 is grossly contaminated. It includes sewage, toilet backups that contain feces, and flood water from outside that carries soil and debris from rivers and creeks. It requires protective equipment, removal of porous materials and cleaning and disinfection.

Exposure can cause serious illness. If you suspect Category 3, keep people and pets out and call a professional. The sewage backup page and the flood damage page explain what that work involves.

Categories at a glance.
CategoryTypical sourcesUsual approach.
1 (clean)Supply line, faucet, tankExtract and dry, clean quickly.
2 (gray)Washer, dishwasher, sump overflowRemove padding, clean, treat, dry.
3 (black)Sewage, creek or river floodingRemove porous materials, disinfect, dry.

Why the category can change

Category is not fixed. Clean water that sits for 48 hours or more can become gray, and gray water can degrade further. Warm temperatures speed this up. Water that passes through a dirty crawl space or a soiled floor can be reclassified. This is another reason time matters.

What it means for you

  • Higher categories usually mean more removal and a higher cost.
  • Soft items such as mattresses and sofas are typically discarded after Category 2 or 3.
  • Insurance coverage can depend on the source, and sewer backup often needs a specific endorsement.
  • Do not handle Category 3 water without protection.

Ask the crew which category they assigned, why and what that means for the scope. A written scope should reflect it. If the source was a burst line, the burst pipe page explains the usual clean-water response.

How crews test and decide

Classification starts with the source. A technician asks where the water came from, how long it has been there and what it touched. They look at the water itself, the materials it soaked and whether it passed through anything soiled. Sometimes the answer is obvious, as with a sewage backup, and sometimes it takes a closer look, as with water that came through a crawl space.

When in doubt, a crew treats the water as more contaminated, because the cost of being wrong in that direction is lower.

What happens to materials in each category

  • Category 1: carpet, pad and drywall can often be dried in place if the response is quick.
  • Category 2: carpet pad is usually removed, and carpet may be cleaned and dried or replaced.
  • Category 3: porous materials such as carpet, pad, insulation and low drywall are removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected.

These are general patterns. A crew will tell you what applies to your home, and the written scope should show it. If you have an infant, an elderly person or someone with a weakened immune system in the house, say so, because it affects how careful the cleanup and clearance should be.

Keep people and pets out

Until a crew has assessed it, treat water of unknown origin as contaminated. Do not wade in it barefoot, do not run a household vacuum on it and do not let children or pets play in it. Wash hands after any contact and wash clothing that touched it.

Finally, ask for the category in writing and for the reason behind it. A clear scope names the category, the materials affected and the steps planned for each. If the crew changes the category, the scope should change with it, and you should hear why before the work continues.

If water is already in the house, do not wait on the paperwork. Call (740) 231-9132.

Key takeaways

  • Category 1 is clean, 2 is gray and 3 is black water.
  • Category can worsen with time and temperature.
  • Higher categories mean more removal and more cleaning.
  • Treat unknown water as contaminated until a crew checks it.

Local note for Marysville, OH homeowners

After heavy rain, creek and surface water that enters a basement is treated differently from water from a supply line. Union County documents logjams on Mill Creek that act like dams, and local news reports describe flooding along Big Darby Creek near Plain City in 2017. Water from outside is generally treated as Category 3 because of what it carries.

If water in Plain City or Raymond came from a yard or street, say so when you call. The source is the first thing the crew asks about.
