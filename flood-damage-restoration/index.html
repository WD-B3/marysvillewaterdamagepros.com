Emergency Flood Damage Cleanup in Marysville, OH

Floodwater is contaminated and moves fast. Dispatch is open 24/7 and starts with safety.

(740) 231-9132See all services

Floodwater in a home is an emergency and a safety problem at the same time. Stay out of the water, keep children and pets away, and do not touch outlets, cords or appliances that are standing in it. If the water is near your electrical panel, call the utility or 911 before anything else, then call our 24/7 dispatch line for the cleanup. Floodwater is treated as contaminated until it is proven otherwise.

This page covers rising water from creeks, groundwater and surface runoff. Wind-driven rain that enters through the roof or walls is a different loss, described on the storm water page, and a wet basement from a failed sump pump is on the basement page.

When flooding is an emergency

Moving water, water still rising, water above outlets and water that came from a creek or drain are all emergencies. So is any water that has covered flooring for more than a day, because porous materials absorb contaminants. Do not run a household vacuum or try to dry carpet yourself with fans if the water was contaminated.

  • Leave the area if the water is still rising or the structure feels unsafe.
  • Do not enter flooded rooms with energized outlets or panels. Wait for the utility.
  • Avoid contact with the water. Wear waterproof boots and gloves if you must go in.
  • Photograph the water line on the walls and the damage to contents.
  • Call your insurer and ask whether flood coverage applies to your policy.

Why flooding is a different kind of loss

Floodwater picks up soil, fertilizer, fuel and sewage as it moves, which puts it in Category 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. That changes the job. Carpet, pad and soaked drywall are usually removed, not dried, and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized. Anything porous that touched the water is treated with caution, including insulation and upholstered furniture.

Flooding history around Marysville

Marysville was founded along Mill Creek, and the county reports that 33 of the creek's 37 miles lie in Union County. In 2017 the county found a logjam about 1,600 feet long and later identified hundreds of logjams or debris piles along the creek. The county says logjams act like dams, backing up water and slowing stormwater drainage.

Rain has produced flooding in the area more than once. Local news reported Big Darby Creek overflowing and flooding McKitrick Park in Plain City in 2017, high water across parts of the county and closed roads in 2018, and flooding from rain on melting snow in 2019. Those events are history, not forecasts, but they show why homes near creeks and low roads should check the flood map.

Check your flood zone

The county lists flood insurance rate maps from 1991 for Marysville and notes that maps are updated over time. Look up your address on the FEMA flood map and ask the Union County Engineer, the county floodplain administrator, about local floodplain rules before you plan repairs.

How the cleanup works

Crews extract water first, then remove materials that cannot be cleaned, such as soaked carpet and pad, wet insulation and lower drywall. Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, and the structure is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are dry. Contents are sorted into what can be cleaned and what cannot. Water extraction and structural drying both play a part.

What affects the price of flood cleanup

Floods vary more than most losses, so the visible conditions decide the scope.

  • How high the water rose. Depth sets how much drywall and insulation must come out.
  • Contamination. Category 3 water adds protective equipment and sanitizing.
  • Time in the structure. Water that sat for days damages more materials.
  • Contents. Cleaning or replacing furniture, appliances and belongings is separate.
  • Rebuild. Replacing walls, flooring and finishes comes after drying.
