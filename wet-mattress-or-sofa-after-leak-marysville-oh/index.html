Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak in Marysville? Keep or Toss

Some furniture can be saved and some cannot. The source of the water and how long it sat decide which.

(740) 231-9132See all services

By the Marysville Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

A leak does not only wet floors and walls. Mattresses, sofas, chairs and rugs soak up water quickly, and they dry slowly. The question people ask first is whether they can be saved, and the honest answer is that it depends on where the water came from, how long the item stayed wet and what it is made of.

This guide helps you sort items into keep, dry and discard, and explains why the answer changes from one item to the next.

Start with the water source

Clean water from a supply line is the best case. Furniture exposed to clean water for a short time can often be dried. Water from a washing machine drain, a toilet overflow or a sewer backup is a different matter, because it carries bacteria and contaminants that do not wash out of foam and fabric.

Soft items touched by contaminated water are usually discarded. The sewage backup page covers that situation, and the categories guide on this blog explains the three water types in more detail.

Mattresses

Mattresses are dense, layered and difficult to dry through. If a mattress got a small amount of clean water on the surface and you act within hours, standing it on its edge, blotting, using fans and a dehumidifier and keeping it in a dry room can work.

If it soaked through, stayed wet for a day or more, or was exposed to contaminated water, it is generally safer to replace it. Moisture trapped inside a mattress invites odors and mold.

Sofas and upholstered furniture

Fabric over foam and a wood frame holds water for a long time. A sofa that took clean water at the base may be saved if it is lifted off the wet floor, the cushions are removed and propped up and air movers push air through it.

Look at the bottom, the frame and the staples, since wet wood can swell, stain or warp. Leather and solid wood pieces often dry better than particle board or upholstery with thick padding.

What to do in the first hours

  • Move items off wet floors and onto dry, raised supports such as blocks or plastic.
  • Remove cushions and bedding and open them up so air reaches the inside.
  • Blot, do not rub, and use fans and a dehumidifier in the room.
  • Photograph everything before you move or discard it.
  • Do not use a heater or direct sun to speed up drying, which can set stains and warp frames.

Keep a list of what was damaged, along with its age and approximate value. If you plan to file a claim, your insurer may ask to see items before they are thrown out. Check your policy for personal property coverage, because it is separate from the building coverage.

If the floor under the furniture was soaked, the carpet and pad may need work too. The carpet water damage page explains how wet padding is handled, and the hardwood page covers what happens when water is trapped beneath a piece of furniture.

Odors and what they mean

A musty smell from a mattress or sofa that was wet means moisture is still inside. Surface cleaning will not remove it, because the odor comes from the layers below. If the smell persists after drying, the item is usually a candidate for replacement. Sprays and fragrances only cover the odor for a short time, and they can make it harder to tell whether the problem is gone.

Special cases

Memory foam and latex hold water for a long time and are slow to dry. Box springs have wood frames and fabric covers that wick moisture and stay damp inside. Leather can stiffen and crack if it dries unevenly. Antique and heirloom pieces may be worth an opinion from a furniture restorer before you decide, since a specialist can sometimes save a piece that looks hopeless.

Children's items need extra care. Stuffed toys, cribs mattresses and car seats that touched anything other than clean water are better replaced than risked, and a car seat that has been soaked may no longer meet the maker's guidance, so check the manual or ask the maker.

Document before you discard

Take a photograph of each item, its label and the area where it sat. Note when you bought it if you remember. Some insurers want to see damaged contents or photos before they are thrown away, so ask first when the item has real value. If the item must go quickly for health reasons, say so in your notes and keep the photographs.

If water is already in the house, do not wait on the paperwork. Call (740) 231-9132.

Key takeaways

  • The water source and the time it sat decide what can be saved.
  • Contaminated water means soft items are usually discarded.
  • Lift furniture off wet floors and dry it with airflow, not heat.
  • Photograph items before you throw them away.

Local note for Marysville, OH homeowners

Furniture donation and disposal rules vary by community. For a bulky item such as a sofa or mattress, check with the City of Marysville Public Service Department, which lists a Streets Division, about large item pickup, and ask whether your trash provider has rules for wet or mold-affected items.

Outside the city, in Raymond or Plain City, the hauler and rules may be different, so confirm before you put an item at the curb.
