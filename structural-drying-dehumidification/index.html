Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Marysville, OH

Water that looks gone is often still in the walls. Drying is the step that decides whether the damage comes back.

(740) 231-9132See all services

Structural drying is the process of removing moisture from the building after the water itself has been cleaned up. It uses moisture mapping, air movers and dehumidifiers to bring walls, floors and framing back to a dry standard. If a wall still feels cool and damp, or a room still smells musty days after a leak, this is the step that is missing.

Walls still damp days after the water was cleaned up?

Many homeowners wipe up a leak, run a fan and assume it is done. Then the baseboard stays cold, the paint bubbles or the room smells stale. That means moisture is still trapped. The trade names for the fix are structural drying and water mitigation, and it relies on measuring moisture instead of guessing by touch.

How moisture is mapped

A crew uses a moisture meter to read drywall, wood, subfloors and concrete, and a thermal camera to find cool, wet areas behind surfaces. Readings are recorded at set points and rechecked daily. The goal is to compare wet materials with a dry reference and keep drying until the readings match. That record also supports an insurance claim.

What the equipment does

Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces and speed evaporation. Dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air so it does not settle back into materials, and low-grain refrigerant units are used for cold, damp conditions. Specialty drying mats pull water from hardwood floors. Wall cavities may be opened or vented so air can reach the wet framing.

How long drying takes

Many jobs take several days, and callers often ask about three or four. Time depends on the material, how long it was wet, the humidity and the temperature. Dense materials like hardwood and concrete dry slowly, and wet insulation can stay wet for a long time. Equipment stays until readings reach the dry standard, not until a set number of days has passed.

Why drying comes before repairs

Closing wet walls in with new drywall traps moisture and invites problems. For that reason, the drying step belongs between water extraction and repair. Once readings are dry, the crew or a contractor repairs drywall, trim and flooring. See wall and drywall repair for what follows.

Questions to ask any drying company

Ask how moisture will be measured and how often. Ask what a dry reading is for each material and what the reference point is. Ask how many air movers and dehumidifiers will be used and why, and how many days they are expected to run. Ask whether wall cavities will be opened or vented.

A good company can answer each question plainly and will record readings on each visit. Ask for a copy of the log at the end. It is the evidence that drying is complete, and it is what an adjuster looks for. Be wary of any drying plan that has no readings in it.

What affects the price of drying

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • Area and materials. More wet surface needs more equipment.
  • Time wet. Older water has soaked deeper.
  • Humidity and temperature. Conditions indoors affect drying speed.
  • Equipment days. Cost relates to how many days equipment runs.
  • Monitoring. Daily visits and readings are part of the job.
