Seasonal Water Damage Risks to Check Every Year in Marysville, OH

Water damage follows the calendar. A few checks each season catch most of it early.

(740) 231-9132See all services

By the Marysville Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

Water losses tend to cluster by season. Winter brings frozen lines, spring brings thaws and heavy rain, summer brings air conditioner condensate and storms, and fall brings clogged gutters and the first cold nights. A calendar of checks is easier to keep than a long list of worries, and each takes only a few minutes.

Winter: keep lines from freezing

Pipes freeze where heat does not reach, such as exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces. Keep the heat on when you travel, let a faucet drip on the coldest nights and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.

Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor spigots in the fall. If you find a frozen pipe, shut off the main before you thaw it. The frozen pipe page covers what happens when a pipe splits.

Spring: thaw and rain

Snowmelt and heavy rain test the sump pump, the grade and the foundation. Check the basement after the first big thaw for damp spots and watch for stains on ceilings that appear after rain. Clean gutters and make sure downspouts are connected and discharging away from the house. Look at the roof for winter damage.

Summer: air conditioners and storms

Cooling systems make condensate, and a clogged drain line can overflow quietly for weeks. Clear the line or have it serviced, and check around attic and closet units. Summer storms bring wind-driven rain and power outages that can stop sump pumps. A walk around the exterior after a storm catches small problems.

Fall: prepare for the cold

Clean gutters again, insulate exposed pipes, drain outdoor lines and test the heating system. Replace old rubber washer hoses and check the water heater for rust or dampness. Fall is also a good time to find the main shutoff and make sure it turns.

Ceilings, floors and walls are where most of these show up first. If you see a stain after rain or a drip near an air handler, the ceiling water damage page explains what to do.

A simple annual calendar

Put four reminders in your phone, one at the start of each season, and give each a single question to answer. In fall, ask whether every outdoor spigot is drained and the gutters are clear. In winter, ask whether any room on an exterior wall is cold enough to worry about.

In spring, ask whether the basement smells or looks different after the thaw. In summer, ask whether the air conditioner drain is running freely. Four questions answered each year catch most preventable losses.

Signs worth acting on between checks

  • A water bill that jumps with no change in how you use water.
  • A ceiling stain that appears or grows after rain.
  • A musty smell in a closet, basement or cabinet.
  • Paint that bubbles or peels on a wall near plumbing.
  • A sump pump that runs constantly or never runs at all.

None of these proves a leak by itself, but each is a reason to look closer. Small signs are cheap to investigate and expensive to ignore. Photograph the sign when you first see it, and photograph it again a week later, since change over time tells you whether water is still arriving.

If you find active damage, the response is the same in every season: stop the source, get standing water out, and dry the structure with readings to confirm it. Seasons change the likely cause and the speed of drying, but they do not change that order.

Weather patterns shift from year to year, so treat the calendar as a floor, not a ceiling. After an unusually heavy storm, a hard freeze or a long power outage, repeat the relevant checks even if the date is off. A quick look inside cabinets, along the basement wall and at the top of the sump pit takes five minutes and often catches a problem while it is still small.

Key takeaways

  • Winter means frozen lines, spring means thaw and rain, summer means condensate and storms.
  • Fall is the time to drain spigots and clean gutters.
  • A stain that grows after rain is a leak until proven otherwise.
  • Four seasonal checks catch most preventable losses.
