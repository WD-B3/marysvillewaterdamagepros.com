Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Marysville, OH

Sewage is a health risk, not just a mess. Dispatch is open 24/7 and starts with safety.

(740) 231-9132See all services

Sewage in a home is an emergency, and the first job is keeping people out of it. Stay out of the water, keep children and pets away, do not use sinks, toilets or the washing machine, and call the 24/7 dispatch line. If water is near an electrical panel or outlets, call the utility or 911 before anything else. Sewage water carries contaminants that make this a Category 3 loss.

Drain downstairs backed up and smells?

Callers put it plainly: the drain in the basement backed up and it is nothing but raw sewage. The trade name is sewage backup cleanup, and it means contaminated water removal, removal of porous materials that touched it, and sanitizing. It is not a job for mops and bleach, and it is not safe to leave it for later.

When sewage backup is an emergency

Always. Any backup from a toilet, floor drain, tub or sewer cleanout is an emergency because the water is contaminated from the start. It becomes more serious when it covers flooring or reaches walls, when it came from a main line shared with others and when multiple fixtures are backing up at once, which suggests the blockage is in the main line.

  • Stop using water in the house, including toilets and washing machines.
  • Keep everyone out of the affected area. Do not walk through the water.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on sewage.
  • Avoid touching surfaces the water reached. Wear gloves and boots if you must go in.
  • Call your plumber or the city if the blockage may be in the main line, and call dispatch for the cleanup.

What causes backups

Common causes are blockages from roots or debris in the lateral line, a collapsed or cracked sewer pipe, and heavy rain that overwhelms the sewer system. Marysville's sewage system has been stressed by flooding before. Local reporting in 2019 described Marysville-owned sewage pumps at Milford Center covered by floodwater, and officials asked residents to reduce sewage use. That is a reminder that backups can follow storms even when no creek flooding reaches your house.

How the cleanup works

The crew wears protective gear, contains the area and extracts contaminated water with equipment made for it. Porous materials the water touched, such as carpet, pad, insulation and low drywall, are removed because they cannot be reliably cleaned. Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, and the structure is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are dry.

Contents are sorted into those that can be cleaned and those that cannot. The work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for Category 3 water. A plumber clears or repairs the line, and the sequence matters because the cleanup should not start until the backup has stopped. See flood damage cleanup for the related case of contaminated creek and surface water.

What happens to belongings after a sewage backup

Porous items that touched sewage, such as upholstered furniture, rugs, mattresses, paper and cardboard, are generally discarded. Hard items made of metal, glass or sealed plastic can often be cleaned and sanitized. Wood furniture falls in between and depends on the finish and how long it was wet.

Photograph items before they go, and keep a list for your insurer. Ask the crew for a written note of what was removed and why. Do not bring items back into the living area until they are cleared. If you have to retrieve something essential, such as medication or documents, wear gloves and boots and put the items in a sealed bag.

What affects the price of sewage cleanup

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • Volume and reach. Larger spreads need more extraction and removal.
  • Materials affected. Removing carpet, pad and drywall adds labor and disposal.
  • Contents. Cleaning or discarding belongings is separate.
  • Safety measures. Containment and protective gear are part of every job.
  • Drying and repair. Drying and rebuilding follow sanitizing.
