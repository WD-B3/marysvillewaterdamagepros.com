Water Damage Restoration in Scott Farms, Marysville, OH

Scott Farms is a newer residential neighborhood in Marysville, centered on Scott Farms Boulevard. Newer homes still leak, and they often have finished space that makes a small leak a bigger job.

Where Scott Farms is

Scott Farms is a residential neighborhood in the City of Marysville, and Scott Farms Boulevard is its main street. The neighborhood is listed on Nextdoor, which tells us residents use the name. In 2019 the Journal-Tribune reported that a new housing development was planned near Scott Farms and was presented to the city planning commission, so the area around it has been growing.

We could not confirm the exact boundaries from a city source, so we describe the area by its street and not by lines on a map. If your address is on a side street, give dispatch the cross street.

Newer homes, and what that does and does not mean

A local agent guide describes Scott Farms homes as dating from the mid-2000s to recent construction, with retention ponds and walking paths. Newer construction means newer plumbing and roofs on average, but it does not rule out a failed hose, a water heater leak or a roof problem. It also tends to mean finished flooring and trim that are expensive to replace, which is a reason to dry quickly.

Whether a given house has a basement is something we cannot say from the neighborhood name. If yours does, the water you meet is more likely to come from a sump pump failure, a window well or groundwater than from a plumbing fixture.

Storm water and ponds

Retention ponds are part of how new neighborhoods manage rain, and the City of Marysville lists stormwater under its Engineering Department. If you see standing water in a yard or street after heavy rain, that is a question for the city. If water gets into the house, it becomes our kind of call. See storm water damage cleanup for what that response involves.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified for this page. Look up your own address on the FEMA flood map and ask the Union County Engineer, the county floodplain administrator, what applies.

Basement and sump pump basics

  • Test the sump pump with a bucket of water before storm season.
  • Check that the discharge line ends outside, away from the foundation.
  • Know whether your pump has battery backup for outages.
  • Keep valuables and electronics off the basement floor.
  • Do not enter a flooded basement while the power is on.

Our basement water cleanup page explains extraction and drying in a finished basement, and water extraction covers the first visit in general.

Practical steps while you wait

Shut off the source when it is safe to reach. Keep people and pets away from water that may be contaminated, such as water from a sump pit, a drain or the outdoors. Photograph the damage, lift furniture onto blocks and open closet doors for air. Do not run a household vacuum on standing water.

Working with your insurer

Ask your insurer what your policy says about the cause of the water, since sudden plumbing failures, sewer backup and outside flooding are often treated differently. Keep receipts and a log of calls. Moisture readings and a written scope from the crew can help describe the work, and they cannot guarantee a claim is paid.
