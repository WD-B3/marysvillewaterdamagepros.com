About the Mill Valley area

Mill Valley is a residential subdivision within the City of Marysville. The Journal-Tribune has described a Mill Valley North subdivision with a neighborhood pool, and has reported that Pulte Homes developed it and gave the city two parcels in 2018. Planning commission coverage in 2022 also mentions a proposal for apartments near Mill Valley, so the surrounding area is still changing.

Real estate listings group several sections under the Mill Valley name, including Mill Valley North, Northwoods of Mill Valley and Woods at Mill Valley. Those labels are marketing names, and we use Mill Valley here the way the city and the local paper do. If your street is near the edge, tell dispatch the cross streets.

Homes built in phases

One local agent guide describes homes in Mill Valley as built from the 1990s through the 2010s, with three and four bedroom houses. We could not confirm basement or crawl space rates from an independent source, so we do not claim them. If you have a basement, it is worth knowing where your sump pump discharges and whether it has backup power.

A house in a subdivision built over a span of years can have different plumbing, roofing and water heater ages from one street to the next. When you call, the age of the equipment that failed is more useful than the age of the neighborhood.

Common sources of water in a suburban house

  • Supply line and valve failures under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Dishwasher, refrigerator and washing machine hoses.
  • Water heater tank leaks and relief valve discharge.
  • Upstairs bathroom leaks that stain the ceiling below.
  • Rain that finds a path through a roof, a window or the foundation.

Appliance and fixture leaks are usually the easiest to stop, because the shutoff is close. Our pages on appliance leaks, water heater leaks and kitchen water damage describe what a visit looks like.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified for this page. Look up your own address on the FEMA flood map and ask the Union County Engineer, the county floodplain administrator, what applies.

What to do before anyone arrives

Stop the water

Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve if you can reach it dry.

Mind the electricity

Stay out of standing water that reaches outlets or appliances, and call an electrician or the utility if you are unsure.

Record it

Photograph the damage and the source before you move things.

Protect what is dry

Lift furniture and small items off wet floors onto dry surfaces.

Drying is a process, not a visit

Pulling water off the floor is the first step. The structure then has to be dried with air movers and dehumidifiers until moisture readings match unaffected areas, which usually takes several days. Ask for the readings, because they are the record of when the structure was actually dry. See structural drying and water extraction for how that works.

Insurance paperwork

Photos, a written scope and moisture readings help describe what happened and what was done. Whether a claim is paid depends on the cause of the water and the terms of your policy, so call your insurer and read the policy before you assume. We cannot decide coverage and do not promise a result.
