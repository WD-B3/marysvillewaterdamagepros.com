Carpet Water Damage Restoration in Marysville, OH

Carpet and pad are judged separately, and the water category decides what happens.

(740) 231-9132See all services

Wet carpet is a race against time. If carpet is soaked in your home, call dispatch, keep foot traffic off it, lift furniture onto blocks and take photos. Whether the carpet can be saved depends mainly on what kind of water soaked it and how long it has been wet.

This page covers carpet and the pad beneath it. The water itself is removed as described under water extraction, and wood floors are on the hardwood floor page.

What decides whether carpet can be saved

Three things matter: the water category, the time and the carpet itself. Clean supply water from a pipe is Category 1, and carpet can often be dried if it is extracted quickly. Water from a washer or dishwasher is Category 2, and carpet may be cleaned or replaced depending on circumstances. Category 3 water from sewage or flooding means carpet and pad are removed.

Water category and what happens to carpet.
Water categoryTypical sourceCarpet outcome.
Category 1Supply line, ice makerOften dried in place if extracted quickly.
Category 2Washer, dishwasher, sumpPad often removed, carpet cleaned or replaced.
Category 3Sewage, flood waterCarpet and pad removed and discarded.

Why the pad matters most

The foam pad under the carpet acts like a sponge. It soaks up water, holds it against the subfloor and takes a long time to dry. In most wet jobs, the pad is removed and replaced even when the carpet is saved. Pad is cheap compared with the carpet and the damage that wet pad can do to a subfloor.

How wet carpet is dried

Crews extract water with a truck-mounted or portable unit, lift the carpet to remove the pad and dry the subfloor. Air movers blow across the carpet and subfloor while dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. The carpet can be reinstalled once readings are dry. Some homeowners found a bit of mold after pulling up wet carpet, so checking the underside is part of the inspection.

If carpet has been wet for several days or more, it often cannot be restored. Replacement is a decision made with moisture readings and photos, and you can ask the crew to show you. See structural drying for the equipment.

Carpet over a slab or in a basement

Carpet laid over concrete slabs or in basements dries more slowly, since concrete holds moisture and basements stay cooler and damper. Tack strips and baseboards also wick water. These areas need more careful drying and moisture checks, including the bottom of the wall.

Mistakes that ruin carpet that could have been saved

Walking on wet carpet pushes water into the pad and spreads dye from furniture legs. Leaving wooden furniture on wet carpet causes stains that do not come out. Leaving the carpet wet overnight with the heat off gives odor a head start.

Another mistake is steam cleaning too soon, which can add heat and moisture to a pad that is already wet. If you can, lift small furniture, put foil or plastic under heavy legs and pull the room together so air can move. Then call, since the first day is the most valuable one.

Finally, keep a small cutting of the carpet and a photo of the pad. Your insurer may ask what the material was, and a sample answers the question without an argument.

Safety steps before you touch anything

  • Wear shoes and keep children and pets off wet carpet.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • If the water was not clean, handle the carpet and pad as contaminated and avoid touching it bare-handed.

Professional water damage work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets out the water categories and the drying goals crews work toward. If anyone is at risk from electricity, gas or contaminated water, deal with that first and call dispatch afterward.

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • Water category. Contaminated water often means replacement.
  • Time wet. Longer exposure reduces the chance of saving the carpet.
  • Carpet and pad type. Quality and age affect whether cleaning pays off.
  • Area. More rooms mean more drying time.
  • Replacement. New carpet and pad are separate from drying.
