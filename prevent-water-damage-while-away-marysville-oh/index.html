How to Prevent Water Damage When You Leave Home in Marysville

The worst leaks happen when no one is home to see them. A short checklist before you go reduces the risk.

(740) 231-9132See all services

By the Marysville Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

A leak that runs for an hour is a mess. A leak that runs for a week is a major loss. When you are away for a weekend, a winter trip or a long stay elsewhere, nobody is there to notice a failed hose or a frozen pipe. The steps below are simple and take about twenty minutes, and they target the failures that cause most unattended water losses.

Shut off the water

For a trip of more than a few days, shut off the main water valve. With the main closed, a failed supply line or fixture has nothing to supply it. Open a faucet to drain the lines if you are away in winter, and ask a plumber before draining a system with a water heater.

If you cannot shut off the main for the whole trip, at least close the valves to the washing machine and the ice maker.

Winter: keep the heat on

Set the thermostat to a safe temperature, often at least 55 degrees, so pipes in walls and cabinets stay above freezing. Open cabinet doors on exterior walls, disconnect garden hoses and make sure the furnace is serviced. Consider a smart thermostat or a temperature sensor that alerts your phone. The frozen pipe page explains what happens when a line splits.

Appliances and fixtures

  • Replace old rubber washer hoses with braided stainless hoses.
  • Do not run the dishwasher or washing machine while you are away.
  • Check the water heater for rust, drips or leaks and consider a drain pan.
  • Make sure the sump pump works, and consider a battery backup.
  • Clear gutters and downspouts before you go.
  • Consider water leak sensors under sinks, near the water heater and by the washer.

Ask someone to check in

Ask a neighbor, friend or family member to walk through the house every few days. Give them the location of the main valve and your phone number. Smart sensors that send alerts to your phone and to a second contact are inexpensive and can catch a leak within minutes.

If you come home to water

Do not walk through standing water if it could reach electrical outlets or appliances. Shut off the main valve if you can reach it safely, take photographs and call dispatch. See water extraction and structural drying for what happens next. Notify your insurer promptly. Some policies have conditions for vacant or unoccupied homes, so read yours before you travel for an extended period.

Smart devices that help

Several kinds of devices can reduce the risk. Leak sensors sit on the floor and send an alert when they get wet. Smart shutoff valves close the main automatically when a sensor trips or when flow looks unusual.

Smart thermostats alert you if the house gets too cold. These devices need working internet and power, so test them before you travel, replace batteries on schedule and make sure alerts go to more than one person.

For extended absences

If you will be away for weeks or months, such as for a winter away from home, consider having the water professionally shut off and the lines drained, and ask your insurer about vacancy rules. Some policies limit coverage for water damage if a home is unoccupied beyond a set number of days, so ask before you go.

Arrange for mail pickup and for someone to inspect the house on a schedule.

A short list for the person checking in

  • Walk every room and look at the ceilings, floors and baseboards.
  • Open the cabinets under sinks and look for dampness.
  • Check the basement and sump pit.
  • Check the thermostat and confirm the heat is running in cold weather.
  • Call you and the dispatch line at the first sign of water.

Do one last walk through before you lock the door. Look under the kitchen sink, behind the toilet and around the water heater, and confirm the washing machine is off and the hoses are tight. Take a photo of each as a reference, because a picture from the day you left is the best comparison if something changes while you are gone.

If water is already in the house, do not wait on the paperwork. Call (740) 231-9132.

Key takeaways

  • Shut off the main water for longer trips.
  • Keep the heat on in winter and test the sump pump.
  • Use leak sensors and ask someone to check in.
  • Read your policy for vacancy conditions.

Local note for Marysville, OH homeowners

Marysville has had storms, snowmelt and creek overflows documented by local news, including flooded roads in 2018 and rain on melting snow in 2019. If you travel in a wet season, check the sump pump and gutters just before you leave. Keep in mind that a power outage can stop a pump while you are away.

If you live in Raymond or Plain City, make sure whoever checks the house knows how to reach you and where the shutoff is. The City of Marysville Public Service Department can help with questions about the city water line.
