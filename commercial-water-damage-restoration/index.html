Water damage in a business is an emergency because every hour of downtime costs money and wet materials get worse. If water is spreading in an office, shop, restaurant or multi-unit building in Marysville, call the 24/7 dispatch line, shut off the water at the source or the main valve if it is safe, and keep staff and customers out of wet areas. Do not run equipment that is standing in water.

When a commercial leak is an emergency

It is an emergency when water reaches inventory, electronics or electrical panels, when floors are slippery for customers, when a leak from one unit is dripping into another, or when sewage is involved. Businesses with tenants below or beside the loss need to act quickly, because a leak that starts in one space often becomes someone else's problem within minutes.

  • Shut off the water at the source or at the main valve.
  • Move inventory, files and electronics out of the wet area if it is safe.
  • Keep customers and staff away from wet floors and from water near panels.
  • Notify the property manager or landlord and your insurer.
  • Photograph the damage and record the time it started.

How commercial jobs differ

Commercial losses involve larger areas, more people and more paperwork than a house. Crews plan around business hours, keep part of a space open when possible and coordinate with a building manager. Large floors may need truck-mounted extraction, and open ceilings, suspended tiles and ductwork are common in commercial buildings. Multi-unit buildings add questions about which space is responsible.

Documentation for the claim

Commercial insurance often asks for more documentation: photos, moisture readings, a log of work done and a description of lost use. We keep these records during the job. Your commercial policy decides what is covered, including business interruption, so read it or ask your agent early. Tenants should check their lease for who is responsible for what.

Common commercial causes

Causes include burst supply lines, failed water heaters, roof leaks, sprinkler line failures, clogged drains and sewage backup. Flat commercial roofs can pond water and leak into the ceiling below. A leaking rooftop air conditioner can do the same. Related pages on burst pipes, ceiling leaks and sewage backup explain the individual causes.

How the cleanup works

The crew extracts standing water, removes wet materials that cannot be saved and sets up drying equipment. Moisture is mapped across the space, including walls, ceilings and floors. Daily readings continue until the building is dry. Contents are moved or protected as needed.

The aim is to return the space to use as quickly as safely possible. Where part of a building can reopen, the crew works with you on containment. See structural drying for the process.

Plan before the next loss

Businesses that handle water losses well usually prepared a little beforehand. Make sure someone on every shift knows where the main water shutoff is, where the electrical panel sits and who to call after hours. Keep a one-page contact list that includes the property manager, your insurer and dispatch.

Photograph inventory and equipment periodically, and store the images off site. Know which valuables can be moved quickly. If you lease space, read the lease for who handles what after a leak, since responsibility for walls, ceilings and flooring is often split between landlord and tenant. A short conversation before a loss is easier than a long argument afterward.

Safety steps before you touch anything

  • Clear people from wet areas and mark slippery floors.
  • Shut off power to affected circuits and keep staff from server rooms and panels that have water near them.
  • Photograph inventory and equipment before anything is moved.

Professional water damage work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets out the water categories and the drying goals crews work toward. If anyone is at risk from electricity, gas or contaminated water, deal with that first and call dispatch afterward.

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • Size of the affected area. Larger floor area means more equipment and time.
  • Water category. Contaminated water adds containment and sanitizing.
  • Building materials. Suspended ceilings, carpet tile and concrete dry differently.
  • Access and hours. Working around operations affects the schedule.
  • Documentation. Commercial claims often need extra records.
