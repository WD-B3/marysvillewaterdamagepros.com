Emergency Burst Pipe Water Damage in Marysville, OH

A burst supply line can release a lot of water in minutes. Dispatch is open 24/7 and starts with shutting it down.

(740) 231-9132See all services

A burst pipe is the kind of loss where minutes matter. Shut off the main water valve first, then call the 24/7 dispatch line, then open a faucet on the lowest level to drain the line. If water is near electrical outlets or a panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 before you call us. Everything else can wait until the water has stopped.

This page is about the damage from a pressurized supply line that split, failed at a joint or let go at a fitting. Pipes that froze are covered on the frozen pipe page, and leaks that are not under pressure, like a slow drain, are a different job.

When a burst pipe is an emergency

It is an emergency whenever water is running and you cannot stop it, when water is falling through a ceiling, or when the main valve will not close. It is also an emergency if the pipe burst in a wall and you only see the stain, because water keeps traveling inside the cavity. Calls like these often come from a pipe under a kitchen sink or behind a bathroom wall.

Stop the water

Close the main valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house. Learn where it is before you need it.

Cut power if it is safe

Turn off breakers to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel while standing dry. Otherwise call the utility.

Protect what you can

Lift furniture, take up rugs and move electronics out of the area.

Document everything

Photograph the pipe, the water and the damage before cleanup begins.

Why supply lines fail

Supply lines fail at fittings, at old solder joints and where a rubber or plastic connector has aged out. Higher water pressure, vibration from an appliance and a past freeze can all weaken a line over time. Older homes around the Uptown square may still have original copper or galvanized lines. Newer homes tend to fail at plastic fittings and braided connectors instead.

What the cleanup involves

The crew starts by confirming the water is off and finding every area the water reached. Water travels, so a pipe burst in one wall often wets the floor beneath it, the ceiling below and the insulation between. Moisture meters and a thermal camera map that path before anything is removed.

Standing water is extracted, wet insulation and drywall that cannot dry in place are cut out, and air movers and dehumidifiers go in. Drying continues with daily readings until the structure reaches its dry standard. Repairs to drywall, paint and flooring come after, and they are a separate scope from the mitigation. The related pages on wall and drywall repair and structural drying show what each stage looks like.

The plumber and the restoration crew do different jobs

Fixing the pipe is plumbing work, and it is not done by the restoration side. A plumber repairs or replaces the line. The restoration crew makes the property safe, removes the water and dries the structure. Call both if you need to, and tell dispatch whether the leak is stopped so the crew arrives prepared.

What affects the price after a burst pipe

The factors are visible once someone is on site.

  • How long water ran. A line that ran for ten minutes and one that ran overnight are very different jobs.
  • Rooms and floors reached. Water that dropped through a ceiling doubles the scope.
  • Materials. Hardwood, carpet, drywall and insulation each dry or fail differently.
  • Drying days. Equipment time depends on humidity and how wet the wall cavities are.
  • Rebuild. Replacing drywall, flooring and paint is separate from drying.

The scope and price are explained before work starts. Call (740) 231-9132.

Sudden and accidental pipe failures are often covered under homeowners policies, while gradual leaks often are not. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and ask what documentation they want before you discard anything.
