Standing water is an emergency because every hour it sits, it moves into drywall, subfloors and furniture. If water is on the floor in Marysville right now, call the 24/7 dispatch line. Then stop the source if you can do it safely, shut off the main water valve if a line is still running and keep people and pets out of the water.

Water extraction is the first step of every wet job, and it is the step that decides how much of the rest you will need.

This page covers one specific job: removing standing water with pumps and extraction units. It is separate from structural drying, which dries what the water soaked, and from flood damage cleanup, which deals with creek and groundwater flooding.

When standing water is an emergency

Water that is deeper than a shallow film, water that is still arriving, and water that reached the second layer of anything porous all count. So does water in a finished basement, in a room with a floor furnace or electrical panel, or in a unit with neighbors below. Call even if the leak has stopped, because the water that is already inside keeps working.

  • Do not wade through water that touches outlets, extension cords or an electrical panel. Call the utility or 911 first.
  • Turn off the main water valve if a supply line is still running.
  • Lift furniture legs onto blocks and take rugs and small items out of the water.
  • Keep children and pets away. If the water came from a drain or toilet, treat it as contaminated.
  • Take photos and short video before anything is moved.

How water is removed

Crews use submersible pumps for depth, truck-mounted or portable extraction units for carpet and hard floors, and wet vacuums in tight spaces. Water comes out in this order: standing water first, then water held in carpet and pad, then water pressed out of the lower few inches of wall. A thermal camera and moisture meter confirm where moisture is still sitting after the visible water is gone.

Extraction does not make a room dry. It removes what can be pumped or vacuumed so the drying equipment has less to do. A floor that looks clear may still be saturated underneath, which is why extraction is almost always followed by drying and daily moisture readings.

What the water is determines the plan

A broken supply line puts Category 1 water on the floor, and it can often be removed and the materials dried in place. Washing machine overflow or sump discharge is Category 2. Sewage and creek flooding are Category 3, and those require removal of porous materials and sanitizing, not just pumping. The categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and the sewage backup page covers the contaminated end of the scale.

Water type and extraction approach.
SourceCategoryExtraction approach.
Supply line, ice maker, tub overflow1Pump or vacuum, then dry in place when caught early.
Washer, dishwasher or sump discharge2Extract, remove soaked pad and low drywall as needed, clean surfaces.
Sewage, rising creek or groundwater3Extract with contamination controls, remove porous materials, sanitize.

Local conditions that put water on the floor

Marysville sits on Mill Creek, and the county has documented logjams that back water up and slow drainage. Heavy rain has also stressed sewer pumping in the area. Local reporting in 2019 described Marysville-owned sewage pumps at Milford Center covered by floodwater. Basement floor drains and sump pumps are where that stress shows up first in a home, so a power outage during a storm is a common trigger for a call.

What affects the price of extraction

Price depends on facts that are visible on site, not on a flat number quoted over the phone.

  • Depth and volume. More water and a larger area mean more pump time and more equipment.
  • Water category. Contaminated water adds protective gear, disposal and sanitizing steps.
  • Access. Water in a basement with stairs, a crawl space or a locked unit takes longer to reach.
  • What comes next. Many jobs continue into drying and repair, which are separate scopes.
