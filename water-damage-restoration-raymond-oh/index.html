When water is spreading in Raymond

If water is running, rising or falling through a ceiling at a Raymond address, call the dispatch line first and then work through the steps below. Stop the source if you can do it safely, shut off the main water valve and keep people and pets out of wet areas. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, and same-day response is the goal when crews are available.

Distance matters in a rural area, so say clearly which road you are on and whether the house is set back from it. Raymond sits at the crossing of State Routes 347 and 739, and giving the nearest of those roads helps dispatch place you quickly.

Where Raymond is and what that means for water

Raymond is a census-designated place in eastern Liberty Township, Union County, with 280 residents at the 2020 census. It has no municipal government of its own.

The community was platted in 1838 as Newton and its post office opened in 1839. Today it has Raymond Elementary School and a branch library, and it sits at the crossing of State Routes 347 and 739.

Because there is no village hall to call, permit and utility questions go to the county and to whichever provider serves your property. Contact the Union County Building Regulation office before repairs if you are unsure whether a permit applies. Ask your water and sewer provider, or your well and septic contractor, who is responsible for what on your lot.

The water problems that show up in a rural community

Properties outside a municipal service area may use private wells and septic systems, so ask which yours uses. A well pump and pressure tank can leak or fail, and a pressure tank in an outbuilding can flood a floor before anyone notices. Septic backups come up through the lowest drain and are Category 3 water, which is the situation described on the sewage backup page.

Outbuildings, detached garages and barns have their own pipes that freeze when the heat is off, so a split line thaws into a flood nobody sees for days. The frozen pipe page explains what happens and what to do. Farmhouses with older plumbing, stone or block basements and long runs of supply line add more reasons for a slow leak to go unnoticed.

Housing and property types

Rural lots in Raymond range from older farmhouses to newer homes on larger parcels. Older farmhouses often have original plumbing, cellars or partial basements and wood framing that wicks water. Newer homes tend to fail at supply connections, water heaters and appliances. A lot with a long gravel drive can also mean a longer wait for a crew, which is why the first phone call and the shutoff matter.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

Stop the source

Close the shutoff valve at the fixture or the main water valve. If you have a well, switch off the pump at the breaker.

Cut power only if it is safe

Turn off breakers to wet rooms from a dry spot. If the panel is wet, stay away and call the utility.

Protect what you can

Lift furniture and move electronics, papers and keepsakes out of the water.

Call and document

Call the 24/7 line, then take photos and video of the water, the source and the damage.

What affects the price in Raymond

The factors are the same as anywhere, and a few are more pronounced in rural areas.

  • Water source and category. Well, supply, storm and septic water are different jobs.
  • Distance and access. Long drives and gravel roads affect scheduling.
  • Materials. Older framing and flooring respond differently from newer.
  • Drying duration. Basements and cellars stay damp longer.
  • Rebuild. Repairs are priced separately from drying.
