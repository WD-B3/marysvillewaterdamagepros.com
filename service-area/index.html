Water Damage Service Area Around Marysville, OH

Marysville, Raymond, Plain City and the roads and neighborhoods between them.

(740) 231-9132See all services

The service area is built around Marysville, the Union County seat, and the nearby communities that most often call for water damage help. Dispatch is the same 24/7 line for all of them, and the location pages below explain what is different about each place.

Marysville, ZIP codes 43040 and 43041

Inside the city, calls come from every part of town. The Uptown square and the streets around the Union County Courthouse include some of the oldest buildings in the city. Residential streets around Timberview Drive, the area near Marysville High School and the blocks around Memorial Hospital of Union County all use the same dispatch line.

Mill Creek runs through the city, and the county reports that 299 parcels inside Marysville border it. Properties near the creek or its trails, including the stretch near the inflatable dam added in 2009, should look up the flood zone for the address on the FEMA flood map. They can also ask the Union County Engineer, who is the county floodplain administrator, about local rules.

Raymond, ZIP code 43067

Raymond sits at State Routes 347 and 739 in eastern Liberty Township. It has no municipal government, so township roads and county offices matter for permits. The Raymond page covers its farmhouses, its rural lots and what to do when the nearest hydrant is not close.

Plain City, ZIP code 43064

Plain City is a village on Big Darby Creek at U.S. 42 and State Route 161, and it lies in both Madison and Union counties. That changes who handles permits, so the Plain City page explains how to find out which side of the line a property is on.

Other nearby communities

Dispatch also takes calls from Milford Center (43045), Richwood (43344), Magnetic Springs (43036) and Broadway (43007), and can discuss work just beyond them. Coverage near the edges depends on crew availability that day, so call and ask instead of assuming.

What to expect from dispatch

Every call begins with the source of the water and any safety risk. After that, dispatch explains what a crew will do first and what the scope and price conversation looks like. The services overview lists every water damage page by cause if you want to read ahead.

Marysville neighborhoods

Within the city, dispatch has written pages for five neighborhoods: Historic Uptown, Mill Valley, Scott Farms, Adena Pointe, Green Pastures. Each page covers only what could be verified about that area and says so where it could not.

Which office handles permits and flood questions

Inside Marysville, the city lists Planning and Zoning for permits, Engineering for stormwater and Public Service for the water and wastewater divisions. Outside the city, Union County has a Building Regulation office, and the Union County Engineer is the county floodplain administrator. Plain City is a village, so its own officials may be involved, and which county a property sits in can change the answer. Ask before you plan rebuild work.

Creeks and why they matter here

Mill Creek in Marysville and Big Darby Creek near Plain City both appear in local flood reporting. Big Darby Creek overflowed and flooded McKitrick Park in 2017, and rain on melting snow caused flooding in 2019. These are reminders that water can reach a property from outside as well as from a pipe. Water from a creek is treated differently from water from a supply line, and the flood damage page explains how.

A note on arrival times

Travel time depends on where the crew is when the call comes in, on road conditions and on how many emergencies are running that day. Dispatch will give you a realistic window when you call and will not promise an exact minute. In the meantime, the steps that matter most are the ones you can take yourself: stop the water if you safely can, stay away from electricity and photograph the damage.

Rural lots and village streets

Rural properties and village streets pose different challenges. A farmhouse outside Raymond may rely on a well and a septic system, and a sump discharge may run across a lot instead of into a street drain. A house on a Plain City street may share storm drains that the village owns. In both cases, the first question is the same: where did the water come from and where is it going now.

Describe your road and the nearest crossroads when you call, because dispatch is matching you to a crew by travel route and not only by ZIP code. A cross street, a landmark or the name of a nearby business all help.
