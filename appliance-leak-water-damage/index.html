Emergency Appliance Leak Water Damage in Marysville, OH

Most appliance floods come from a hose or valve, not the appliance. Dispatch is open 24/7 for the cleanup.

(740) 231-9132See all services

An appliance leak turns into an emergency quickly when the supply line keeps running. Close the shutoff valve behind or beside the appliance, unplug it only if you can do so without standing in water, and call the 24/7 dispatch line. If the valve will not close, shut off the main water valve. Stay away from outlets and cords that are wet.

This page covers leaks from washing machines, dishwashers, refrigerators with ice makers, and similar appliances. Leaks from a water heater are on their own page, and cabinet and sink damage in kitchens is on the kitchen water damage page.

When an appliance leak is an emergency

Water that spreads beyond the appliance, water running from a hose that will not stop, and water that has reached a wall or the floor below are all emergencies. One caller described a washer valve that broke and ran for at least an hour before anyone noticed, which is long enough to soak hardwood and reach the room below. Leaks under a refrigerator can hide for weeks behind a kickplate.

  • Close the appliance shutoff valve, or the main water valve if needed.
  • Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and floor are dry.
  • Pull the appliance away from the wall only if it is safe, to see the hoses.
  • Lift rugs and small items out of the water.
  • Photograph the hose, the valve and the water before cleanup.

Where appliances fail

Washing machine hoses, especially rubber ones, can fail at the connection. Dishwasher supply lines and drain hoses crack and loosen. Refrigerator ice maker lines are thin and can kink or split behind the unit. Water heaters and sump pumps are separate. Appliances in upstairs laundry rooms often send water through the ceiling below, which is why the water often shows up first on another floor.

What the cleanup involves

The crew extracts water, then finds where it traveled. Under a washer, water may have run under the baseboard into the wall cavity. Under a dishwasher, it may have soaked the subfloor and cabinet base. Moisture meters and a thermal camera show which areas are wet, and the plan follows.

Hardwood and laminate flooring need prompt drying, as explained on the hardwood floor page, because water pulls under boards and causes cupping. Carpet and pad may be dried if the water was clean and the response was fast, and the carpet page explains how that decision is made.

Clean water, gray water and what changes

Water from a supply line or ice maker is usually clean. Water from a washer drain or dishwasher can contain detergent, food and soil, and it falls in Category 2. Category 2 water means porous materials like carpet pad and baseboards are more often removed. Tell dispatch which appliance leaked so the crew arrives with the right plan.

Mistakes that make appliance floods worse

The most common mistake is wiping up the visible water and stopping there. Water that ran under a washer or dishwasher has often reached the subfloor, the baseboard and the wall cavity, and none of that is visible. A second mistake is leaving wet rugs and cardboard on a wet floor, which keeps moisture in place and stains the finish.

A third is running a household vacuum on standing water, which is a shock risk. Use a wet vacuum made for water, and only on a dry power circuit. Finally, do not push the appliance back into place before the floor is checked. Leave it pulled out so the area can be looked at and dried.

Safety steps before you touch anything

  • Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and floor are dry, otherwise leave it and shut off the circuit from the panel.
  • Close the appliance's own supply valve before the main when you can reach it safely.
  • Do not run the appliance again until a technician has checked the hose, valve or drain.

Professional water damage work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets out the water categories and the drying goals crews work toward. If anyone is at risk from electricity, gas or contaminated water, deal with that first and call dispatch afterward.

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • Time before discovery. An hour of water and a week of seepage are different losses.
  • Flooring type. Hardwood, laminate, tile and carpet respond differently.
  • Water type. Clean supply water and a dirty drain discharge call for different work.
  • Reach. Water that reached a lower floor adds ceiling and wall work.
  • Drying days. Equipment time depends on materials and humidity.
