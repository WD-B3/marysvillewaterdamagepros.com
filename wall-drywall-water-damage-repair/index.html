Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Marysville, OH

Drywall soaks up water and loses strength. Drying and repair are two separate steps.

(740) 231-9132See all services

Wet drywall is weak drywall. If a wall is soaked, stained or bubbling, call dispatch, stop the source, and do not paint over it. Drywall can sag, crumble and hold moisture against framing for a long time, and the damage often extends beyond what you see.

This page covers walls, drywall and baseboards. The cause may be a burst pipe, a leaking appliance or storm water. Ceilings are described on the ceiling page, and the process of drying is on the structural drying page.

What water does to drywall

Drywall is gypsum pressed between paper, and both materials absorb water. When wet, it softens, stains, swells and loses strength. Baseboards wick moisture upward from the floor, and paper facing is a food source for mold if it stays damp. One caller had a huge hole in the kitchen wall from water that came in behind siding, and another saw a soft spot forming in a wall.

  • Stains that grow or have a brown ring.
  • Bubbling, peeling or cracking paint.
  • Soft or crumbling spots when pressed gently.
  • Baseboards that pull away or swell.
  • A musty smell near a wall.

Can wet drywall be dried in place?

Sometimes. If drywall was exposed to clean water and the response was fast, it can occasionally be dried with air movers and cavity drying. If it has been wet for days, if the water was contaminated or if the paper is crumbling, it usually has to be cut out. The decision relies on moisture readings, not on how the wall looks.

How the repair works

The crew measures moisture, flags the wet sections and cuts drywall a little beyond the damaged area, usually along a stud. Wet insulation is removed, and drying equipment runs until framing is dry. Then new drywall is hung, taped, mudded, sanded, primed and painted. Matching texture and paint takes care.

Baseboards and trim are removed to let the wall breathe, and are reinstalled or replaced afterward. A kitchen wall behind a sink or a bathroom wall behind a tub may involve plumbing repairs first. See kitchen water damage for an example.

Hidden moisture in wall cavities

A wall may look dry while the cavity is wet. Thermal imaging shows cool spots, and a moisture meter confirms the reading. Insulation holds water and keeps framing damp. A small test hole or a removed baseboard may be needed to check inside, which is a normal part of the inspection.

Mistakes that hide wall damage

Painting over a stain is the classic one. It looks fixed for a few weeks, and then the stain bleeds through or the paint bubbles. Sealing a wall too early is another. If drywall is closed in over damp framing, moisture stays inside with nowhere to go.

A third mistake is cutting a small patch around the stain without checking how far moisture traveled. Water moves along studs and up the wall by wicking, so the wet area is usually larger than the stain. Use a moisture meter, or have someone use one, and mark the edge of the wet zone before cutting.

What affects the price of wall repair

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • Area affected. More wall means more drywall and finishing.
  • Insulation and framing. Wet insulation must be replaced.
  • Water type. Contaminated water means removal.
  • Finish. Texture and paint matching add work.
  • Drying time. Cavities dry slowly.

Sudden leaks that damage walls are often covered, and long-term leaks and neglect often are not. Read your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph the wall before anything is removed.
