Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Marysville, OH

Wood floors react to water within hours. Early drying often decides whether they can be saved.

(740) 231-9132See all services

Hardwood floors absorb water and then swell, cup or buckle, and the sooner they are dried the better the odds. If water is on your wood or laminate floors, lift rugs and furniture, wipe up what you can, and call dispatch. Do not apply heat or wax, and do not sand a floor that is still wet.

This page is about the floor itself: solid hardwood, engineered wood and laminate. The cause of the water may be a leaking appliance, a burst pipe or an air conditioner line, and each has its own page.

What water does to a wood floor

Wood takes up moisture and expands. When boards absorb water from below, the edges rise and the center sinks, which is called cupping. When moisture is trapped, boards can lift and buckle off the subfloor. One homeowner noticed cupping in walnut flooring after an air conditioner drain line backed up, and another needed hardwoods dried after a washer valve failed and ran undetected for at least an hour.

  • Edges of boards rising into a shallow curve.
  • Gaps opening or closing between boards.
  • Boards lifting off the subfloor.
  • A dark stain or a musty smell at the seams.
  • Finish clouding or turning white.

When quick action matters

Wood begins to change shape within a day or two, so quick extraction and drying are important. The faster moisture is brought down, the more likely a floor settles back flat without sanding. After a long exposure, boards may need replacement. The choice depends on what the moisture readings and the board condition show, not on how the floor looks from above.

How hardwood is dried

Crews extract water from the surface, then use drying mats or panels that pull moisture through the boards. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the room, and moisture meters track the wood and the subfloor. Sometimes a few boards are removed to let air reach the subfloor. The process takes days, not hours, and the readings tell you when to stop.

Repair after drying

Once the wood is dry, cupped floors often flatten over time. If they do not, boards may be sanded and refinished or replaced. A flooring contractor handles that work, and matching old boards requires planning. Patience is important, because refinishing too early traps moisture. The carpet page covers the other common floor covering.

What to do in the first hours

Act quickly and gently. Remove standing water with a wet vacuum, lift rugs and furniture, and wipe the surface dry. Open interior doors so air can move, and use fans pointed across the floor, not at it. Keep the room cool enough that the floor does not dry unevenly in one area.

Do not use heat guns, hair dryers or space heaters on the floor, since fast uneven drying can crack the finish and split boards. Do not nail down cupped boards, and do not seal them with wax or polish. Call dispatch, tell the crew what the floor is made of and how long it was wet, and take photos of the damage before drying starts.

What affects the price of hardwood repair

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • Species and type. Solid, engineered and laminate floors have different outcomes.
  • Area wet. More floor area means more mats and time.
  • Time wet. Longer exposure causes more swelling.
  • Subfloor condition. Water under the boards adds work.
  • Refinishing or replacement. Rebuilding is separate from drying.
