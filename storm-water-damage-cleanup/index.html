Emergency Storm Water Damage Cleanup in Marysville, OH

Wind-driven rain finds every gap. Dispatch is open 24/7 after storms.

Storm water damage is an emergency because rain keeps coming while the damage spreads. If water is entering your Marysville home after a storm, call the 24/7 dispatch line, keep away from electrical hazards and fallen lines, and keep people out of wet areas. If a tree or limb has opened the roof, stay out of the room below and cover the opening only if it is safe.

This page covers rain and wind getting into the building envelope through the roof, windows, siding or foundation. Rising water from creeks is a different problem, described under flood damage cleanup, and a plain roof leak with no storm is covered on the ceiling page.

When storm damage is an emergency

It is an emergency when rain is still entering, when ceilings are sagging, when water is near electrical panels or when a tree has opened the roof. Storm days also put a lot of calls on dispatch at once. Call early, and be ready to describe what you see so the crew can plan the order of work.

  • Stay away from downed power lines and call the utility or 911.
  • Move people out of rooms with sagging ceilings or exposed openings.
  • Cover openings with a tarp only if it is safe to reach them.
  • Shut off breakers to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel while standing dry.
  • Photograph damage inside and outside before any cleanup.

How storm water gets in

Wind-driven rain pushes water under shingles, around windows and doors and through damaged siding. Gutters that overflow can send water down the foundation. Hail and falling limbs create openings. After heavy rain, basements can take in water at the floor joint or through window wells, which is covered on the basement page.

Recent weather history here

Local reporting in 2017 described steady rain and Big Darby Creek overflowing. A flood advisory in 2018 led to closed roads in Marysville, and a 2019 report described flooding from rain on melting snow. These are past events, not predictions, but they show that sudden heavy rain does cause trouble in Union County. If you saw water entering after a storm, call and describe it.

What the cleanup involves

Crews extract water, remove wet insulation and drywall that cannot dry in place and set up drying equipment. Storm water is often Category 2 or 3 depending on where it came from, so protective measures vary. A roofer or contractor handles roof and siding repairs, and the restoration side handles the water damage inside.

Temporary protection, such as a tarp, may be needed until permanent repairs can be made. Keep receipts for temporary repairs, since many insurers reimburse reasonable protection costs under a covered loss.

A short walk-around after a storm

Once it is safe, walk the outside of the house. Look at the roof for missing or lifted shingles, at flashing around chimneys and vents and at gutters and downspouts. Check window and door seals, siding and the grade near the foundation. Inside, look at ceilings, the attic if it is safe to enter and the corners of the basement.

Take photos with a date stamp, including wide shots and close-ups. Small signs, such as a damp stain on a ceiling, can mean a larger problem above. If you see anything, call right away and describe where the water is entering. The faster you document and report, the easier it is to get a clear scope.

Safety steps before you touch anything

  • Stay away from downed power lines and treat every wet outlet as live.
  • Do not wade through standing water outdoors, since it can hide debris and carry contamination.
  • Keep generators outside and away from windows, because exhaust is deadly indoors.

Professional water damage work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets out the water categories and the drying goals crews work toward. If anyone is at risk from electricity, gas or contaminated water, deal with that first and call dispatch afterward.

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • Where water entered. Roof openings and window leaks affect different parts of the house.
  • Water category. Water that touched soil or debris is more contaminated.
  • Area affected. Attics, ceilings and walls add to the scope.
  • Structural damage. Roof or siding repairs are separate trades.
  • Drying time. Wet insulation and framing take time to dry.
