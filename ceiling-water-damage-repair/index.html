Emergency Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Marysville, OH

A wet ceiling can come down without warning. Dispatch is open 24/7, and roof leaks are covered on this page.

(740) 231-9132See all services

A sagging or dripping ceiling is an emergency. Water collects above drywall until the weight is too much, and the ceiling can fall without warning. Keep people out from under it, move furniture aside, and call the 24/7 dispatch line. If water is dripping from a light fixture, turn off the breaker for that room only if you can reach the panel while standing dry. Otherwise stay out and call the utility.

This page covers the ceiling itself and whatever is above it, whether that is a roof, an attic, an upstairs bathroom or a supply line. Because a roof leak ends up as a wet ceiling, roof leaks are handled here and not on a separate page. For wet walls, see wall and drywall repair.

When a ceiling leak is an emergency

A drip is urgent. A bulge or soft spot is more urgent, and a sagging ceiling is an emergency. A hole forming in the middle of a stain means the drywall has lost its strength. Callers often describe an attic that started dripping a little while ago, or a bathroom ceiling that suddenly shows a stain. The longer drywall stays wet, the more it softens and the more insulation above it soaks up.

  • Move people and furniture out from under the wet area.
  • Place a bucket under active drips and puncture a small hole in the lowest point of a bulge only if you can do so safely, to let water drain in a controlled way.
  • Do not step into attic spaces over a wet ceiling.
  • If the ceiling shows a stain after rain, document it with photos and call for an inspection.
  • Shut off the water supply if an upstairs fixture is the source.

Where the water comes from

The common sources are a roof leak through shingles, flashing or a vent, an upstairs bathroom or toilet supply line, a leaking air conditioner or furnace condensate line in the attic, and a plumbing line inside the ceiling cavity. Each source needs a different fix, and the first step is finding out which one it is. The stain pattern, the timing and whether it changes with rain all help.

Roof leaks and water that enters through the roof

A roof leak often shows up away from the actual hole. Water runs along rafters and insulation before it reaches the drywall. After a storm, check for lifted shingles, damaged flashing around chimneys or vents, and stains in the attic along the sheathing. A restoration crew dries and repairs the damage. A roofer repairs the roof, and both are usually needed. A temporary tarp may be needed until the roof can be fixed.

If the leak appeared after a wind or rain event, the storm water damage page explains how storm openings are handled. Wet attic insulation must be removed if it has soaked through, because compressed, wet insulation does not dry well in place.

How a wet ceiling is repaired

The crew measures moisture in the drywall, insulation and framing, then cuts out the sections that cannot be saved. Drying equipment goes in, often with an air mover directed at the open cavity. Once readings show dry, new drywall is hung, taped, textured and painted. Stains do not simply paint over, because tannins and minerals bleed through ordinary paint.

Safety steps before you touch anything

  • Stay out from under a bulging or sagging ceiling, because the water above it can come down at once.
  • Keep lights and fans on the wet ceiling switched off until the area is checked.
  • Place a bucket under a drip and keep people clear of the area until a crew has checked it.

Professional water damage work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets out the water categories and the drying goals crews work toward. If anyone is at risk from electricity, gas or contaminated water, deal with that first and call dispatch afterward.

The factors are visible once the ceiling is opened.

  • Source of the water. A plumbing leak and a roof leak call for different trades.
  • Size of the wet area. A single stain and an entire room are different scopes.
  • Insulation and framing. Soaked insulation must be removed and replaced.
  • Ceiling finish. Textured ceilings are harder to blend than flat ones.
  • Drying time. Cavities above ceilings can be slow to reach a dry standard.
