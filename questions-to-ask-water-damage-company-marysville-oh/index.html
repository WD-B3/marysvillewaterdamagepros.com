What to Ask a Water Damage Company Before You Hire in Marysville

A few questions reveal whether a company will document the work, explain the scope and handle changes openly.

(740) 231-9132See all services

By the Marysville Water Damage Pros team. Published 2026.

When water is on the floor, it is tempting to hire the first company that picks up the phone. Speed matters, but a few minutes of questions can save you from unclear pricing, missing documentation or work that stops before the structure is dry.

The list below is designed to be asked on the phone or at the door. Good companies answer plainly, and you can compare answers across more than one.

Questions about the response

  • How soon can a crew be here, and what will they do first?
  • Is the line staffed around the clock, and who will I talk to?
  • What should I do while I wait?

A company that gives you safety steps on the phone, such as shutting off the water and staying out of standing water near electricity, is thinking about you first. Be careful with promises of exact arrival times before dispatch knows where you are.

Questions about the scope and price

  • Will I get a written scope and price before work begins?
  • What is included, and what is not?
  • How are changes in scope approved?
  • Are drying and monitoring charged by the day or as a package?
  • Is the rebuild part of your work or a separate phase?

Questions about insurance and credentials

  • Are you insured, and can you provide proof?
  • Will you document the work in a way an adjuster can follow?
  • How does payment work, and do you require payment before the work?
  • Do you hold any certifications, and can I verify them?

Do not assume a company will bill your insurer directly. Ask how payment works and keep a copy of everything you sign. Certifications can be a useful sign, but verify them yourself instead of relying on a claim made on a flyer.

Watch for pressure

Be cautious of anyone who asks you to sign a work authorization that gives away your claim rights, pushes you to decide on the spot or refuses to explain what the paperwork says. Read what you sign. It is reasonable to take a few minutes, even in an emergency, to confirm the basics.

To see what a clear scope looks like for common jobs, read the pages on water extraction, structural drying and basement cleanup.

What to have ready when you call

Companies can help you faster when you can describe the situation clearly. Know where the water came from, when it started, how far it spread and whether it is still running. Have your address, your insurance information and a good phone number ready. If you have photos, keep them handy. The better the description, the better the crew can prepare before they arrive.

What to expect on arrival

  1. A safety check and a quick look at the source.
  2. Photographs and moisture readings of the affected areas.
  3. A written scope and price for you to approve.
  4. Extraction, removal of unsalvageable materials and equipment setup.
  5. A plan for monitoring visits until the structure is dry.

You should be able to follow the process from start to finish. A company that cannot explain what it plans to do, or what it will do next, may not have a plan. Ask for a copy of the scope and the readings, and keep them with your insurance records.

After the job

Ask for a final set of readings, a summary of work done and the equipment used. Ask what repairs remain and who will do them. Keep invoices and the log. If something does not match what you were told, bring it up quickly and in writing.

Finally, trust how the conversation feels. A company that listens, explains and does not rush you is usually the same company that documents and communicates during the job. If a call leaves you with more confusion than answers, it is fine to move to the next name on your list, as long as the water has been stopped in the meantime.

Key takeaways

  • Ask for a written scope and price before work starts.
  • Ask how dryness will be measured and logged.
  • Ask what is included and what is not.
  • Take a few minutes to read what you sign.
