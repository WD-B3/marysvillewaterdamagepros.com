Emergency Slab Leak Water Damage in Marysville, OH

A leak under a concrete slab shows up above it. Dispatch is open 24/7 for the water damage.

(740) 231-9132See all services

A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs under or inside a concrete floor, and it can quietly soak flooring and walls above it. If you suspect one, shut off the main water valve, call the 24/7 dispatch line, and stay out of wet areas near outlets. A plumber finds and repairs the leak, and the restoration side removes water and dries the structure.

Warm spot on the floor, or a water bill that jumped?

People rarely say slab leak. They say the floor feels warm in one spot, the water meter keeps moving when everything is off, or there is a damp line along the baseboard that will not dry. Those are the symptoms of a pressurized line leaking beneath the slab. The trade term for the cleanup is slab leak water damage restoration, and it starts with confirming the leak and shutting the water off.

This page applies only to the parts of a home built on a concrete slab, such as an addition, an attached garage or a slab-on-grade house. Check which parts of yours sit on a slab, since a home may also have a basement or crawl space.

When a slab leak is an emergency

It is an emergency when water is visible on the floor, when flooring is lifting, when the water meter is spinning with every fixture off, or when you hear running water you cannot trace. A leak under a slab can run for a long time without being noticed, and the water has to go somewhere. It often rises through cracks and edges and moves into baseboards and drywall.

  • Shut off the main water valve and check whether the meter stops.
  • Look for warm floor spots, damp carpet along walls and cracks with moisture.
  • Do not walk through standing water near outlets or appliances.
  • Photograph the area, including the water meter reading.
  • Call a plumber for leak detection and call dispatch for the water damage.

How the damage is handled

The crew uses moisture meters and a thermal camera to find where water has traveled. Flooring that sits on the slab, especially hardwood, laminate and carpet with pad, is often affected first. Baseboards and the bottom of drywall wick moisture upward and may need to come out. Concrete holds moisture, so drying a slab takes longer than drying framing.

Drying equipment runs with daily readings until the structure reaches its dry standard. A plumber may repair the leak by rerouting a line or opening the slab, and that work usually happens before the final repairs. The hardwood floor page and carpet page cover the two floor types most often affected.

Why a slab leak is different from a burst pipe

A burst pipe in a wall is visible once someone opens the wall. A slab leak is hidden under concrete, so the first problem is locating it, and the damage often spreads sideways through the floor system. A leak can also run for a long time at low volume, which means more materials have absorbed moisture than the visible damage suggests.

How the job usually unfolds

A slab leak job runs in a sequence. First comes the shutoff and the call to a plumber for leak detection. Next the restoration crew maps moisture, removes water and starts drying. The plumber then repairs the line, which may involve breaking into the slab or rerouting the pipe. Drying continues while the repair is made and after it is finished.

Once the structure is dry, flooring, baseboards and drywall are repaired. Because several trades are involved, it helps to keep one list of who is doing what and when. Ask each trade to confirm in writing what they plan to do and how long it will take.

Safety steps before you touch anything

  • Shut off the main water valve as soon as you suspect a slab leak.
  • Do not walk barefoot on a warm slab floor, because a hot water line can scald.
  • Keep electrical cords and appliances off any wet floor.

Professional water damage work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets out the water categories and the drying goals crews work toward. If anyone is at risk from electricity, gas or contaminated water, deal with that first and call dispatch afterward.

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • How long the leak ran. Long-running leaks soak more material.
  • Flooring type. Hardwood and carpet over a slab need different drying plans.
  • Area affected. Water spreads through the floor system and up the walls.
  • Drying duration. Concrete slabs release moisture slowly.
  • Repair access. The plumber's repair method affects the schedule for rebuilding.
