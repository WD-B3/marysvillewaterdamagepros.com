Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Marysville, OH

Cabinets, sink bases and kitchen floors hold water in places you cannot see.

(740) 231-9132See all services

Kitchen water damage usually begins under the sink or behind an appliance, and it hides inside cabinets and under flooring. If water is spreading across the kitchen floor, shut off the valves under the sink or the main water valve, unplug nearby appliances only if you can do it dry, and call dispatch. A kitchen has more materials that react to water than almost any other room.

This page covers the room itself: cabinets, countertops, sink bases, floors and walls. The failure of a dishwasher, refrigerator or washing machine is covered on the appliance leak page, while a burst line is described on the burst pipe page.

What kitchen water damage looks like

Callers describe a burst pipe under a sink with water all around on the kitchen floor and a water lake across the room. In one case, water from siding had rotted the drywall and left a huge hole in the kitchen wall. The signs are swollen particleboard cabinet bases, peeling laminate, buckled floors, a musty smell and cabinets that pull away from the wall.

  • Swollen or crumbling particleboard in the sink base.
  • Floor that feels soft, lifts or has gaps.
  • Dark stains on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher.
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer close squarely.
  • Water spots on the ceiling of the room below.

Why cabinets are the hard part

Most kitchen cabinets are built with particleboard or MDF, which swell when wet and rarely return to shape. Solid wood and plywood cabinets hold up better if dried quickly. Countertops and the cabinet faces often survive, but the base can fail. A crew checks which parts can be dried in place and which must be removed, before deciding on repair or replacement.

What the cleanup involves

Water is extracted, toe kicks are removed so air can reach the cabinet bases and the wall behind is checked with a moisture meter. Drying mats and air movers dry the floor, and dehumidifiers pull moisture from the room. If the flooring is hardwood or laminate, drying starts quickly to limit cupping, as the hardwood floor page explains.

Once the kitchen is dry, repairs begin. These may include replacing a cabinet base, patching drywall, replacing flooring and repainting. Matching cabinets and finishes can take planning, so ask for a written scope that separates drying from rebuilding.

Common causes in kitchens

Supply lines and shutoff valves under the sink, leaking drain traps, dishwasher connections, refrigerator ice maker lines and window leaks above a sink all appear in kitchen losses. A slow drip under a sink can wet the base for weeks. Check under the sink once in a while and watch for rusting fittings or a smell of damp wood.

Where else to check after a kitchen leak

Water from a kitchen leak travels. Look at the floor along the edge of the cabinets, where baseboards meet the floor and in the next room. If the kitchen is above a basement or a crawl space, check the ceiling or the underside of the floor for dripping or staining.

Open the cabinet next to the one that leaked and look at the back panel. Check under the dishwasher by removing the kickplate, and behind the refrigerator if there is an ice maker line. Run a hand along the wall behind the sink for coolness or softness. Anything that feels cool or damp deserves a moisture reading, and a thermal camera is a quick way to scan the whole room.

Safety steps before you touch anything

  • Turn off the dishwasher, disposal and range hood circuits if water reached outlets.
  • Do not use a gas range if water reached the control area, and call the gas utility if you smell gas.
  • Move food from a refrigerator that sat in water and treat it as unsafe if in doubt.

Professional water damage work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets out the water categories and the drying goals crews work toward. If anyone is at risk from electricity, gas or contaminated water, deal with that first and call dispatch afterward.

The visible conditions decide the scope.

  • Cabinet material. Particleboard often needs replacement and plywood can often be dried.
  • Flooring. Tile, vinyl, laminate and hardwood respond differently.
  • Time wet. Longer exposure damages more.
  • Water category. Clean supply water and drain water differ.
  • Rebuild scope. Cabinet matching and finishes drive the rebuild cost.
